## Prospective design

Freeze 72 synthetic factual snapshots: 12 each for nose-versus-road orientation,
yaw direction, absolute wheel-command semantics, radians-to-degrees conversion,
nearest native wheel angle and nominal 15 m pursuit-formula execution. Sign
families include five mirrored pairs and two distinct exact-zero controls.
Other families use six mirrored pairs. Geometry is generated by the unchanged
Python simulation; reflected fixtures use the clockwise stadium for this static
exercise. These are declared artificial snapshots, not recorded trajectories.
The user-selected live track remains the 24 m stadium with 12 m/s speed limit
and 0.12 rad maximum wheel angle.

Each case receives the same full `road_navigation` observation under two question
contexts. `minimal` supplies only the explicit factual task. `guide` prepends
the exact production `damped` text as clearly delimited reference background;
the identical static task explicitly takes priority in both conditions. Only
question instructions differ between these paired conditions. This evaluates
context interference under that framing, not length alone or an exact replica
of the production driving question. Existing factual labels, including yaw
direction, remain in the observation: success can reflect reading those facts,
and does not prove independent numeric reasoning.

Factual answer menus contain three directional facts, four numeric conversions
or five physical wheel angles. The formula asks for the stated equation's
quantized result, not an optimal driving input. It uses the actual `ego_x_m`
and `ego_y_m` from the 15 m preview row; arc-ahead 15 does not imply x=15.
Boundary ties are avoided. Expected answers and proof metadata are never
transmitted. Native-wheel classes are not perfectly balanced (3/3/2/2/2), so
report class counts and family results rather than treating pooled accuracy as
a universal capability estimate.

A separate bridge reuses the twelve formula fixtures with the unchanged fifteen
native geometry-menu descriptions. It prescribes a pedal rather than asking
what is best for driving: four cases each require acceleration, coast or brake,
with matching pedals across each geometric mirror pair. Score joint choice,
wheel and pedal separately. The host supplies no calculated target or preferred
native choice; all calculation and selection are the model's task.

Two repetitions yield 288 factual and 48 bridge calls, **336 calls maximum**.
Shuffle the 84 case/block units with fixed order seed 20261019, pair the two
contexts, then reverse unit order and swap context order on repetition two.
Responses are not seeded. Repetitions measure stability of the same fixtures;
they are not independent new examples. Count endpoint failures in attempted
accuracy and keep unrun cases explicit. Mirror consistency alone is not accuracy.
No controls are applied, no race is started, and static accuracy cannot qualify
closed-loop driving or establish safe/optimal steering.

## Error diagnosis and its limits

Across all 96 formula-related responses, 90 selected moderate wheels, five
selected full wheels and one selected straight. Of 32 expected-full responses,
28 selected moderate; of 16 expected-straight responses, 15 selected moderate.
Only three of eighty nonzero-target responses used the opposite sign, all in
the minimal native bridge. This points toward trouble producing the required
magnitude on these composite-rule questions; the isolated nearest-angle task
was correct on all 48 responses. It does not establish which calculation or
interpretation failed.

There is a material confound: all twelve formula fixtures currently hold
moderate wheels with sign matching the nominal target. Chosen wheels matched
that current setting on 87/96 responses. The experiment cannot distinguish
copying the held input from a general preference for moderate steering or
another heuristic. A future contrast must vary current wheel independently of
target geometry; this bank was kept frozen after discovery of that limitation.

The coarse five-angle labels also fail to discriminate two specific shortcuts:
all twelve expected answers stay the same if x is incorrectly replaced by 15,
or if atan is omitted. Correct answers therefore do not prove correct preview
extraction or precise trigonometry. Existing categorical yaw/road facts and
familiar wheel-angle conversions can also support answers without independent
arithmetic. A follow-up should include fresh discriminating values and vary
current wheel, yaw and target independently before testing a revised prompt live.

These synthetic formula poses cover six mirrored patterns on the initial
straight, with fixed 8 m/s speed and 9 deg/s signed yaw. The earlier 149/486
closed-loop agreement count covers a different, temporally correlated state
distribution and a real racing objective. Its guide was an instruction, while
this guide is quoted background under an explicit quiz override. Do not equate
these percentages or infer an effect on rescue rate.
