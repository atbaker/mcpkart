# Free feasibility screen: prompt-only cruise speed under longer delays

Prospective registration before these simulations, October 20, 2026. The
question-local prompt passed eight clean fresh starts at ordinary observed
latency. Isolated long replies in those runs do not establish tolerance for
sustained slow decisions. Test a physically modest prompt hypothesis before
spending on model comparisons: ask the pedal question to coast at or above
10 m/s when the existing edge/off-road concern is false. Keep the speed<2
acceleration priority, existing concern predicates, and concern braking/coast
rules unchanged. All wheel rules and thresholds remain unchanged.

This phase is **free host-guide simulation, not Jev performance**. Compare
the unchanged fixed guide with the fixed 10 m/s cruise guide at starts
45–50, alternating standard and clockwise. Keep the 24 m road, 12 m/s
physical maximum, 0.12 rad steering, Python engine, three laps and existing
three-second rescue. A slower chosen pedal is not a change to the physical
maximum. No future-state prediction, host control or evaluated concern flag
is to enter any later paid request.

Fixed delay profiles, expressed as physics ticks until the first affected
step: 16, 31, 46 and 61 ticks (250/500/750/1000 ms plus one 60 Hz step),
and a periodic sequence of nineteen 16-tick replies then one 91-tick reply.
Each profile starts at its first slot for each start and cycles as needed.
Hold the previous native input during the delay. Discard a reply if the kart
was rescued meanwhile. End on finish, three times that start's deterministic
reference, or the same forward-preview/required-gate domain exit used by the
prompt. Keep all 60 cells, including failures. Save commands, outcomes and
source/plan hashes; verify the real model ledger is unchanged.

A candidate may motivate a separately registered native/model comparison
only if all six ordinary 16-tick controls are clean and useful, it gains at
least four clean finishes net over the other 24 stress cells, loses no useful
finishes overall, and gains at least two clean finishes among the six fixed
46-tick starts. These are engineering-feasibility selection criteria, not
proof that Jev follows the new pedal rule or that the guide is optimal.
If any criterion fails, retain the failure and do not launch a paid governor
comparison under this registration. Any later study needs its own fresh
native rule screen, exact timed controls, fixed order and model-outcome gate.
No paid request is admitted by this document. No application default changes.

## Results

The physical-selection gate failed. All 60 free cells and the separate
saved-command/physics audit completed: 163,937 exact physics steps and 7,813
query actions reconstructed. No model requests or real ledger changes occurred.

| Delay profile | Unchanged clean/useful | Cruise 10 clean/useful |
|---|---:|---:|
| 250 ms + frame | 6/6 | 6/6 |
| 500 ms + frame | 0/6 | 1/6 |
| 750 ms + frame | 0/6 | 0/6 |
| 1,000 ms + frame | 0/6 | 0/6 |
| Nineteen 250 ms, then 1,500 ms, each + frame | 3/6 | 5/6 |

In this table every useful finish was also clean. Other cells ended at the
prompt's declared domain boundary. The candidate gained three clean stress
finishes net, below the required four, and gained zero at 750 ms, below the
required two. It retained all six ordinary controls and lost no useful finish
net, but those partial successes do not pass the gate. No paid cruise study
was admitted. The 12 m/s physical maximum was unchanged throughout.

These are exact offline engineering-guide simulations under exogenous delay
sequences, not model runs or an exact wall-clock reproduction. They suggest
a physical control-policy limitation under sustained delay, even with perfect
execution of the guide. They do not show how Jev would respond to a different
prompt, establish a precise latency threshold, or identify the cause of any
specific recorded race's failure. The successful actual model cohort remains
separate evidence at its observed latency.
